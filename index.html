05 — THE ANSWER

Enough clues

With signals from several satellites, your phone can find the location that fits all of the measurements.

So what just happened?

Your phone didn't
ask the satellites.

The satellites simply sent signals. Your phone measured how long those signals took to arrive and used that information to work out where it must be.

The slightly nerdier version

It all comes down
to time.

01

A satellite sends a signal

GPS satellites continuously broadcast signals containing the time they were sent and information about the satellite's position.

02

Your phone receives it

The signal travels through space at almost the speed of light before reaching your phone.

03

Your phone measures the delay

The tiny difference between sending and receiving tells your phone roughly how far away the satellite is.

04

More satellites make it precise

Your phone combines several measurements to find the position where they all make sense together.

One important detail

Real GPS
is 3D.

Our animation used circles on a flat screen because they're easier to understand.

In reality, the Earth is three-dimensional, so GPS works with distances in three dimensions.

And there's another problem: your phone's clock isn't accurate enough on its own. That's why real positioning normally uses at least four satellites.
